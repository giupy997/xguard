BLOCKED
A match against a malicious-address list, or an undeclared instruction that changes ownership, authority or delegation. The wallet still asks you to sign.
CAUTION
A reversible but undeclared change, an unlimited approval, or a counterparty with no history. Whatever you proceed with, GUARDIAN keeps watching.
CLEAR
The payload matches the declared intent, every program is verified, no authority or delegate changes. A Clear verdict is a statement about the payload, not about the token you are buying.
02
02 The threat Instruction names as decoded by build 0.4. Rule ids are stable.

Your wallet shows you a button. The chain executes the payload.

A Solana transaction is a list of instructions. The page shows you a button. Your wallet shows you a summary. Neither one is the payload, and drainers live in that gap. In 2024, wallet drainers took roughly $494M across chains (ScamSniffer, 2024 annual report). Every one of those transactions was signed by its victim.

Six instructions that empty wallets

System.AssignR-014
Hands ownership of your wallet account to another program. Dressed up as "Claim airdrop".
Token.SetAuthorityR-021
Changes who owns a token account, or who can mint. Dressed up as "Swap".
Token.Approveamount: 18446744073709551615R-003
An unlimited delegate: that number is u64::MAX. Dressed up as "Enable trading". Drained later, when you have stopped paying attention.
Token.CloseAccountdestination ≠ youR-008
Closes a token account and sends the balance and the rent to someone else. Dressed up as "Reclaim rent from empty accounts".
Durable noncedelayed broadcastR-033
A transaction you signed in March, broadcast in June, when the balance is worth taking. Dressed up as "Verify wallet ownership".
Lookalike originreal routeR-050
A real Jupiter route built by jup-ag.app instead of jup.ag, with one instruction appended at the end. Dressed up as the site you meant to visit.

None of these need your seed phrase. They need your signature, once.

03
03 How it works Latencies are beta measurements on the fixture corpus.

Three readings before the signature. One after.

I

Simulate and diff

GUARDIAN decodes the unsigned transaction instruction by instruction and runs simulateTransaction on the RPC you configure, with signature verification off and the blockhash replaced, so the result reflects the chain as it is now, not a third party's summary. It records every balance change, every authority change, every account closed or assigned, then diffs that against the declared intent. If the page says "Swap" and the simulation shows your USDC account changing owner, the verdict is Blocked, with the instruction number and the rule that fired.

II

Match against intel

Every counterparty address, program and origin is checked against the SlowMist malicious-address database, public STRIDE findings and a crowdsourced phishing-domain list refreshed every ten minutes. A program's age, verified source and upgrade authority are scored too, so a three-hour-old program with no source is flagged before anyone has reported it. A match explains itself: who flagged it, when, and how many reports.

05
05 Trust Fail-open is deliberate: no agent, no verdict, your wallet as before.

What GUARDIAN can see, and what it can't.

Can

  • Read your public address and on-chain state.
  • Decode and simulate the unsigned transaction your wallet is about to display.
  • Prepare a Revoke transaction for you to sign.
  • Send the alerts you asked for, to the channel you chose.

Cannot

  • Read your seed phrase or private key. There is no field for it and no code path to it.
  • Sign, submit, modify or delay a transaction.
  • Move funds.
  • Stop you. The final decision is always yours; a Blocked verdict is evidence, not a lock.
  • See a program's future upgrade. A verified program can turn malicious after an upgrade, which is why GUARDIAN keeps watching after the signature.
DATA PATH
Decoding and simulation happen on your machine, against the RPC you configure. The decoded transaction and its simulated effects, never a key, go to the agent, which runs inside a trusted execution environment and returns the verdict with its rule id. If the agent is unreachable, your wallet behaves as before and shows an "unverified" state. Fail-open is deliberate.
TEE
Each release publishes an attestation you can verify against the binary, so the code that read your transaction is the code that was published, and nobody can change it silently, GUARDIAN's operators included.
OPEN RULES
The detection rules and the decoder ship under Apache-2.0 in Q3 2027. The intent-diff grammar and the fixtures behind the demo above are public from the first release. The intel feeds and the TEE build are published as attestation reports, not source.
NOT A WALLET
GUARDIAN is an adapter for Phantom, Solflare and Backpack. Your wallet stays your wallet, your keys stay where they are. Remove it and your wallet works exactly as before.

09
09 Questions

Questions a careful person asks.

Does GUARDIAN need my seed phrase?

No. It never asks and has no field for it. It reads the unsigned transaction your wallet is about to display and the public state of the chain.

Can it block a transaction without my consent?

No. It shows a verdict with evidence; your wallet still asks you to sign. Blocked is advice, not a lock.

What happens if the service is down?

Fail-open is deliberate: your wallet behaves as before and shows an "unverified" state. Known-malicious matching keeps working from lists cached on your machine for 24 hours. You lose freshness, not your wallet.

Why a TEE?

So the agent that sees your pending transactions cannot be modified silently, by anyone. Each release publishes an attestation you can verify against the binary.

What exactly is open source?

The rule set and the decoder, under Apache-2.0 from Q3 2027. The intel feeds and the TEE build ship as attestation reports, not source, so an attacker cannot test against the exact matching logic.

What if GUARDIAN is wrong?

You override with a one-line reason. The override is logged and becomes a test fixture, so the rule gets a counter-example and false positives go down for everyone.

Which wallets?

Phantom, Solflare and Backpack adapters ship in Q2 2027. Any wallet that exposes the unsigned transaction can be supported.

Does it slow down signing?

Simulation adds 180–400 ms in the beta build. The verdict renders before the wallet modal finishes animating.

Why 0.1 SOL and not dollars?

Because what you are protecting is denominated in SOL, and because a card form on a security product is a phishing vector we would rather not create. Billed on-chain from the protected wallet; cancel by not paying.

Is there a token?

No. There will not be one. A security product that depends on its own token price has the wrong incentives.
