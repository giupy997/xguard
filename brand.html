xguard watches. You decide.

A Mac goes wrong quietly: a protection switched off and forgotten, a program that starts at every login, an installer Gatekeeper would have refused, a key anyone can copy. xguard is the agent that keeps reading those places, every few minutes, and says in one plain sentence what changed and whether it matters. Then it stops, because the decision is yours. The whole brand is that one gesture: an editor's red pen on a typeset page. It marks what is wrong. It never rewrites the page.

Large serif headlines that state facts. A sans for the body. A mono for anything that is literally on the Mac: a path, a program, a command, a setting. Exactly one chromatic color, used only to say that something is wrong. The site is laid out like a security whitepaper that somebody at Stripe designed: numbered sections, hairline rules, ledger tables instead of cards, a recorded terminal session instead of a demo video, and a hero that is two short sentences with a red full stop.

Restraint is the trust signal. A product that never raises its voice reads as a product that knows what it is looking at.

The one rule · auditable on every screen

The only color on the site is the one we use to say no.

If a pixel is red (--pen), something is wrong at that pixel: a finding of medium severity or above, the status column of a flagged startup item, a form error, the ✕ and the ring on a misuse plate, the single flagged element in a diagram, and the full stop of the hero sentence, which is the editor's mark. Red is never used for a CTA, never for the logo, never for decoration, never for "brand". Everything else is ink and paper. When in doubt, it is not red.

Surfaces
No cards, no shadows, no gradients, no canvas, no particles, no glows, no blur.
Icons
None, except the mark, the caret glyph, the check mark in a status column, and arrows drawn as 1 px lines.
Motion
No section-entrance animations. Sections are simply there, like pages of a document. Motion is reserved for reading.
Claims
Every claim on the page is pinned to a file path, a program name, a code signature, a date, or a command the reader can run.

Positioning

For people who use a Mac every day and distrust being sold protection, xguard is the free, always-on security agent that watches the places where a Mac goes wrong: its protections, its startup items, its open ports, its downloads, its keys. It explains each finding in plain language and changes nothing until they approve it. Unlike an antivirus (a black box with a subscription) or a one-time checklist (true for an afternoon), xguard keeps watching, shows its evidence, needs no account and no password, and leaves the decision to the owner of the Mac.

The promise · footer, canonical, verbatim

It watches. You decide. Free, with no account.

One line in the footer, three sentences, in this order. Never reordered, never shortened, never followed by a fourth.

An x with one arm lifted out.

A lowercase x set as two strokes of type. The arm that would finish it, top right, is lifted out and held as a single square point on its own axis.

Read as a letter, it is the x of xguard. Read as a gesture, it is a cross that is not finished: three arms are drawn, the fourth is held back until you place it. The agent reads, marks and waits; the last stroke is yours. The mark says "x", it says "not closed yet", and it never says "shield", "lock" or "alarm".

Paper (#EDEBE6) on ink, always currentColor. The strokes and the point are one color.

Construction

64-unit master, stroke 8, every coordinate on the 2-unit grid. Grid in --rule, extension and dimension lines in --rule-strong, the stroke's centerline dashed in ink. Numbers in circles are keyed to the list below.

1Stroke
8 units on a 64 grid, one eighth of the box. Butt caps on every end; the 45° ends are part of the drawing, never rounded.
2Backslash
Centerline from (12, 12) to (52, 52): the full diagonal of the 40-unit square, drawn in one stroke.
3Slash
Centerline from (12, 52) to (34, 30). It crosses the backslash at the center (32, 32) and stops one half-stroke past it, so the crossing reads as an x, not a chevron.
4Lifted arm
An 8 × 8 point at x 44..52, y 12..20, centered on (48, 16): exactly on the slash's axis, where the fourth arm would end.
5Gap
14 units along the axis, from the slash's cap to the point's near corner at (44, 20). 3.5 px at 16 px, so the arm never rejoins the x on fractional device-pixel ratios.
6Bounds
The centerlines span 12..52 on both axes; with the caps the ink spans about 9..55. The 64-unit artboard's border is the clear space.
Grid
Every coordinate sits on a 2-unit grid. Rendered at 16, 20, 28, 32, 64 and 200 px the strokes land on whole or half pixels.

Clear space and minimum sizes

x = 8 units = one stroke width = one eighth of the mark's height, measured from the ink. The 64-unit artboard already includes the clear space: keep the SVG box empty.

Clear space on every side is one stroke width at the rendered size, one eighth of the mark's height, measured from the ink: 2 px at 16 px, 8 px at 64 px, 25 px at 200 px. Nothing enters it. Because the master's strokes are inset, the artboard's own edge is the clear-space line. The wordmark's gap in a lockup is defined separately and is always larger.

Mark alone
16 px minimum, and only as the favicon cut.
Mark in a lockup
20 px minimum, the nav size.
Lockup width
110 px minimum, from the mark's left edge to the wordmark's last letter.

The mark never ships alone except as the favicon and the seal. On the page it always has the wordmark next to it or beneath it.

Ink, paper, and one red pen.

Eleven values. Nine of them are grey. The tenth and the eleventh are the pen and its wash, and they appear only where something is wrong.

--ink-0#0A0A0BPage background. Warm near-black, never #000.
--ink-1#121214Raised surface: the terminal, code blocks, inputs.
--ink-2#1A1A1DHover and active rows and tabs, expanded-row insets. Never decorative.
--rule#2A2A2EDecorative hairlines only: section rules, table rows. 1.38:1, exempt from AA.
--rule-strong#626268Boundaries that must be perceivable: input borders, ghost buttons, meaningful diagram strokes, the tab track. 3.27:1 on ink-0. Not on ink-2.
--paper#EDEBE6Primary text, headlines, the Low chip outline, the logo.
--paper-2#A8A6A0Secondary text, body on panels, check glyphs.
--paper-3#85837EEyebrows, metadata, footnotes. 12 px minimum. Prefer ink-0 or ink-1 beneath it.
--paper-hi#FFFFFFPrimary button hover fill, and nothing else.
--pen#FF5A4EEverything that is wrong. Text on a pen fill is always ink-0.
--pen-wash#2A1512The solid tint behind a flagged row. Opacity-free, so it survives screenshots.

Display craft

A high-contrast serif on near-black blooms: thin hairlines sparkle and break. Therefore:

Weight
450 for H1 and H2; the site loads that one static instance of Newsreader, so a 500 is never synthesized.
Optical size
font-optical-sizing: none; font-variation-settings: "opsz" 48 for H1 and H2. Do not let the browser pick opsz 72 at 104 px.
Tracking
H1 −0.025em, H2 −0.015em. Never looser.
Italics
None loaded, none synthesized: font-synthesis: none on html. Emphasis is a sentence break, a mono span, or a hairline.
Rendering
text-rendering: optimizeLegibility; -webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased on html. Fallback faces carry size-adjust and ascent overrides so the hero keeps its line count with the fonts blocked: CLS target 0.

Twelve columns. The first three are the margin.

The Manuscript grid. A 1180 px container, twelve columns with 24 px gaps from 1024 px up. Columns 1–3 hold the editorial apparatus; columns 4–12 hold the text. Nothing is centered except the footer colophon.

Breakpoints

320–599gutter 20
One column. The margin collapses to one mono line on the section rule. Body stays 17 px; mono steps to 12.5 px under 400.
600–1023gutter 40
One column, the same single line on the rule. The nav turns sticky at 840, where ruled lists gain their 160 px label column.
1024+gutter 64
Twelve columns, gap 24. Margin cols 1–3, sticky at 96 px (nav 56 + 40). Headlines cols 4–11; full-width devices cols 4–12.

The section rule

A 1 px --rule across the full container with the number on it, flush left, on an --ink-0 patch that interrupts the line. Below 1024 the number and the running title share the rule as one line; the number is never printed twice.

Motion is reserved for reading.

The page does not animate on scroll, on load, or on hover, except as listed here. Opacity and transform only, one easing everywhere, and no loops.

Easing
--ease: cubic-bezier(.2, .8, .2, 1) everywhere, with no exceptions on the current site.
Properties
opacity and transform, and nothing else.
Never
No requestAnimationFrame loops, no canvas, no parallax, no scroll-jacking, no hover scale. Section headings do not fade in. Diagrams do not draw themselves. Numbers do not count up.

State the mechanism, not the feeling.

The product never raises its voice. It names the file, the program or the setting, gives the number with its unit and its source, and admits the limit in the same breath as the claim.

01
State the mechanism, not the feeling. "Runs read-only checks every ten minutes and changes nothing without your approval", never "keeps you safe".
02
Prefer the path to the metaphor. If a metaphor is used, the file, the program or the setting follows in the same sentence.
03
Short declarative sentences. Full stops. No exclamation marks. No rhetorical questions in body copy; FAQ questions are questions, that is fine.
04
Every number carries its unit and its source. "About a second per scan, measured on an M-series MacBook."
05
Admit the limit in the same breath as the claim. "xguard is not an antivirus and does not scan file contents."
06
Banned words. The list is below. There are no exceptions for quotes, testimonials or partner copy.
07
Second person, present tense. The product is "xguard" or "it". "We" appears only where humans are speaking: the footer and the GitHub issues.
08
Paths are shown as typed. ~ for the home folder, the user name never shown, hashes truncated 4…4 with the full value in a title attribute, and never a path invented without a real file behind it.
09

Details / summarydetails.faq-item
Does it need my password or admin rights?

No. Every check runs as you. Fixes that need admin rights, like turning on FileVault, are printed as steps for you to run.

Does it send my data anywhere?

Without an Anthropic key, nothing: xguard makes no network requests. With a key, each new finding is sent once to the Claude API under your account. Never the contents of your files.
