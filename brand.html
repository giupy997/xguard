01 / The idea

GUARDIAN is the proofreader.

A Solana transaction is a document your wallet asks you to sign, and almost nobody reads it. GUARDIAN is the proofreader. It reads the document aloud in plain language, marks the line that lies, and hands it back before you sign. The whole brand is that one gesture: an editor's red pen on a typeset page. Large serif headlines that state facts. A sans for the body. A mono for anything that is literally on-chain. Exactly one chromatic color, used only to say that something is wrong. The site is laid out like a security whitepaper that somebody at Stripe designed: numbered sections, hairline rules, ledger tables instead of cards, precise line-art diagrams, and a hero that is a single sentence with a red full stop.

Restraint is the trust signal. A product that never raises its voice reads as a product that knows what it is looking at.

The one rule · auditable on every screen

The only color on the site is the one we use to say no.

If a pixel is red (--pen), something is wrong at that pixel: a flagged instruction, a mismatched simulation cell, a Blocked or Caution chip, a form error, the single flagged node in a diagram, and the full stop of the hero sentence, which is the editor's mark. Red is never used for a CTA, never for the logo, never for decoration, never for "brand". Everything else is ink and paper. When in doubt, it is not red.

Corollary 1
No cards, no shadows, no gradients, no canvas, no particles, no glows, no blur.
Corollary 2
No icons except the mark, the caret glyph, the check glyph, the status glyphs in the roadmap, and arrows drawn as 1 px lines.
Corollary 3
No section-entrance animations. Sections are simply there, like pages of a document. Motion is reserved for reading.
Corollary 4
Every claim on the page is pinned to an instruction name, a program id, a rule id, a list provenance, a date, or a balance diff.

Positioning

For Solana holders who sign transactions every day and distrust being sold to, GUARDIAN is the non-custodial defense layer that sits between the wallet and the signature: it decodes and simulates every pending transaction, diffs what the page says against what the payload does, matches every counterparty against threat intelligence with the provenance shown, and keeps watching approvals after the signature. Unlike a wallet's built-in warning (a label) or a hosted screening API (a verdict you cannot audit), GUARDIAN shows its reading row by row, cites the rule that fired, never touches a key, and leaves the decision to you.

02 / The mark

A G whose loop never closes.

A squared G whose loop never closes. The corner that would close it is lifted out and held as a single square point, one stroke width away.

Read as a letter, it is the G of GUARDIAN, built like a block of set type. Read as a gesture, it is a sentence held open: the point is not placed until the line has been read. The crossbar that enters from the right is the line between the user and the signature. The mark says "G", it says "not closed yet", and it never says "shield", "lock" or "alarm".

Construction

64-unit master, stroke 8, every coordinate on the 2-unit grid. Drawn here at 320 px: one unit is 5 px. Grid in --rule, dimensions in --rule-strong, centerline dashed in ink.

Stroke
8 units on a 64 grid, one eighth of the box. Butt caps, miter joins. Outer bounds 8..56 on both axes.
Corners
Three corners are true arcs of radius 6 on the centerline: 10 outer, 2 inner. The fourth, where the right stem meets the crossbar at (52, 36), is a square miter whose outer corner sits at (56, 32), echoing the square point.
Point and gap
The top stroke ends with a butt cap at x = 40. The point occupies x 48..56, y 8..16: exactly the band the missing corner would fill. The gap between stroke and point is 8 units, one stroke width, 2 px at 16 px, so it never closes on fractional device-pixel ratios.
Crossbar and mouth
The crossbar enters at y = 36 and stops at x = 34, just past the center of the counter. The mouth, from the point's bottom at y = 16 to the stem's top at y = 32, is 16 units: generous at every size.
Grid
Every coordinate sits on a 2-unit grid. Rendered at 16, 20, 24, 32, 64 and 200 px the strokes land on whole or half pixels.

Clear space and minimum sizes

x = one stroke width = one eighth of the mark's height. Nothing enters the band.

Clear space on every side is one stroke width at the rendered size, one eighth of the mark's height: 2 px at 16 px, 8 px at 64 px, 25 px at 200 px. Nothing enters it. The wordmark's gap in a lockup is defined separately and is always larger than the clear space.

05 / Color

Ink, paper, and one red pen.

Eleven values. Nine of them are grey. The tenth and the eleventh are the pen and its wash, and they appear only where something is wrong.

--ink-0#0A0A0BPage background. Warm near-black, never #000.
--ink-1#121214Raised surface: the demo panel, code, inputs, the message card.

Display craft

A high-contrast serif on near-black blooms: thin hairlines sparkle and break. Therefore:

Weight
450 for H1 and H2 on the variable axis; 500 for tier names and the sparse act at 32–40 px.
Optical size
font-optical-sizing: none; font-variation-settings: "opsz" 48 for H1 and H2. Do not let the browser pick opsz 72 at 104 px.
Tracking
H1 −0.025em, H2 −0.015em. Never looser.
Italics
None loaded, none synthesized: font-synthesis: none on html. Emphasis is a sentence break, a mono span, or a hairline.
Rendering
text-rendering: optimizeLegibility; -webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased on html. Fallback faces carry size-adjust and ascent overrides so the hero keeps its line count with the fonts blocked: CLS target 0.

07 / Voice

State the mechanism, not the feeling.

The product never raises its voice. It names the instruction, cites the rule, gives the number with its unit and its source, and admits the limit in the same breath as the claim.

01
State the mechanism, not the feeling. "Runs simulateTransaction against the RPC you configure", never "keeps you safe".
02
Prefer the instruction name to the metaphor. If a metaphor is used, the instruction name follows in the same sentence.
03
Short declarative sentences. Full stops. No exclamation marks. No rhetorical questions in body copy; FAQ questions are questions, that is fine.
04
Every number carries its unit and its source. "$494M across chains in 2024 (ScamSniffer, 2024 annual report)."
05
Admit the limit in the same breath as the claim. "Blocked is advice, not a lock."
06
Banned words. The list is below. There are no exceptions for quotes, testimonials or partner copy.
07
Second person, present tense. The product is "GUARDIAN" or "it". "We" appears only in the waitlist section and the footer, where humans are speaking.
08
Addresses are truncated 4…4. First four, ellipsis U+2026, last four, the full key in a title attribute, and never invented without a fixture behind them.
09
Program and instruction names are set in mono, exactly as decoded. System.Assign, Token.SetAuthority, Token.Approve, Jupiter v6.sharedAccountsRoute, ComputeBudget.SetComputeUnitLimit.
10
GUARDIAN is always uppercase in running text and the wordmark. Never "Guardian", never "guardian" except in a domain.
11
No italics anywhere. Emphasis is done with a sentence break, a mono span, or a hairline.

Details / summarydetails.faq-item
Does GUARDIAN need my seed phrase?

No. It never asks and has no field for it. It reads the unsigned transaction your wallet is about to display and the public state of the chain.

Can it block a transaction without my consent?

No. It shows a verdict with evidence; your wallet still asks you to sign. Blocked is advice, not a lock.

Hairline top border, summary in Geist 500 20 with the caret at the right, native marker hidden, 12 px above the answer and 24 px below it. Keyboard-operable by default; no JS. The caret turns from pointing down to upright over 200 ms. Also the mobile nav menu.
